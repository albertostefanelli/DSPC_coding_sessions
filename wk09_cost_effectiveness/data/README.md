# W9 data sources

Both files are built by [`prepare_wk09_data.py`](prepare_wk09_data.py) in this folder, which downloads the original replication files and prints checks against the published tables.

- `six_city_canvassing.csv`: the results of six nonpartisan door-to-door canvassing experiments in local elections in November 2001 (Bridgeport, Columbus, Detroit, Minneapolis, Raleigh, St. Paul), from Donald P. Green, Alan S. Gerber and David W. Nickerson (2003), "Getting Out the Vote in Local Elections: Results from Six Door-to-Door Canvassing Experiments," *Journal of Politics* 65(4): 1083–1096, Table 2 (p. 1092). Replication data: ISPS Data Archive, Yale, [doi:10.60600/YU/B9TZNZ](https://doi.org/10.60600/YU/B9TZNZ), version 2.0, file `GreenGerberNickerson_JP_2003-1_EDITED.tab` (file id 27228), retrieved 2026-11-06. License: CC0 1.0.
  - One row per city plus the paper's pooled row, `All sites`. Values are transcribed from Table 2 as printed (one decimal).
  - Columns: `city`; `people` (registered voters in the experiment, from the table's n); `itt_pp` and `itt_se_pp` (post-treatment difference in 2001 turnout between the treatment and control groups, and its standard error, in percentage points; this is the effect per person assigned to be canvassed); `contact_rate` (share of the treatment group actually contacted, "Percentage of Treatment Group Actually Contacted" divided by 100); `late_pp` (the table's "Effects of Actual Treatment on Voting in 2001", the effect per person contacted, in percentage points).
  - Check: `prepare_wk09_data.py` re-estimates each city's ITT from the replication file with walk-list fixed effects and reproduces the printed ITTs to rounding (4.04, 1.38, 2.42, 1.87, 0.07, 4.64). Contact rates in the file differ from the printed ones by up to 0.6 points (for example Raleigh 45.2% against 44.6%); the README in the deposit says the file was edited in 2020 to reduce disclosure risk and results can differ slightly. The notebook uses the printed values.
  - Price used in the notebook: the authors' own costing in the conclusion (p. 1094): "Suppose one were to hire campaign workers at a rate of $10 per hour ... If we imagine that the average canvasser makes eight contacts per hour, the cost per vote would be $15." $10 ÷ 8 = $1.25 per contact (2001 dollars). Footnote 8 notes that a complete accounting must add the fixed costs of the organizations that recruit canvassers. The authors' $15 rests on "12 successful face-to-face contacts" per vote; the pooled LATE of 7.1 points in their Table 2 implies about 14, which is why the notebook's $17.6 differs from $15.

- `ggl_2008_mailers.csv`: turnout by mailer in the August 2006 Michigan primary election experiment of Alan S. Gerber, Donald P. Green and Christopher W. Larimer (2008), "Social Pressure and Voter Turnout: Evidence from a Large-Scale Field Experiment," *American Political Science Review* 102(1): 33–48, [doi:10.1017/S000305540808009X](https://doi.org/10.1017/S000305540808009X). Replication data: ISPS Data Archive, Yale, [doi:10.60600/YU/CGMWNW](https://doi.org/10.60600/YU/CGMWNW), version 2.0, file `GerberGreenLarimer_APSR_2008_social_pressure.tab` (file id 42238, 344,084 individuals), retrieved 2026-11-06. License: CC0 1.0.
  - One row per experimental group, in the paper's order: Control, Civic Duty, Hawthorne, Self, Neighbors (codes 0, 2, 1, 4, 3 in the file's `treatment` column, as labelled in the authors' R script).
  - Columns: `mailer`; `households` (number of distinct `hh_id`; households were the unit of random assignment); `people` (registered voters in those households); `voted` (how many of them voted in the primary, from `voted`).
  - Check: the counts reproduce the paper's Table 1 (99,999 control households, about 20,000 per mailer) and Table 2 (191,243 control individuals; turnout 29.7%, 31.5%, 32.2%, 34.5%, 37.8%).
  - Price used in the notebook: the paper (p. 38): "The powder blue mailings used here were printed on one side and cost 30 cents apiece to print and mail. Treating each experimental group therefore cost approximately $6,000. The "Self" mailing generated 1,854 votes at a rate of $3.24 per vote. The "Neighbors" mailing generated 3,106 votes at $1.93 per vote." One mailing went to each household.
  - Exercise 3's later rounds of the Hawthorne mailer (each round half as effective as the one before) are an assumption made up for the exercise, not data.

The earlier Week 9 files (`gotv_tactics.csv`, `new_canvassing_study.csv` and a copy of the Kling and Stratmann robocall extract) were removed in November 2026; the notebook no longer uses them.
