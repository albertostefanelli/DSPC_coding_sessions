# W8 data sources

- `final_polls_1998_2022.csv`: a filtered copy of FiveThirtyEight's pollster-ratings poll archive, [`pollster-ratings/raw_polls.csv`](https://github.com/fivethirtyeight/data/blob/be387bc1649413be1c85f2181b2c32aacee24315/pollster-ratings/raw_polls.csv) at commit `be387bc1649413be1c85f2181b2c32aacee24315` (January 2024), retrieved 2026-10-28. Licence: [CC BY 4.0](https://github.com/fivethirtyeight/data/blob/master/LICENSE); codebook in [`pollster-ratings/README.md`](https://github.com/fivethirtyeight/data/tree/master/pollster-ratings).
  - Rows kept: polls with `time_to_election <= 21`, `type_simple` in Pres-G, Sen-G, Gov-G, House-G, `cand1_party == "DEM"` and `cand2_party == "REP"`. Four polls with joint or independent sponsors (`partisan` = `DEM,REP`, `REP,DEM`, `IND`) were dropped. 8,589 polls, cycles 1998–2022.
  - Columns kept and renamed: `cycle`, `race`, `location`, `race_type` (from `type_simple`: President, Senate, Governor, House), `pollster`, `methodology`, `sponsor` (from `partisan`: DEM → Democratic, REP → Republican, missing → Nonpartisan), `poll_date` (from `polldate`), `sample_size` (from `samplesize`), `margin_poll`, `margin_actual`. Values are otherwise unchanged.
  - `margin_poll` and `margin_actual` are Democrat minus Republican, in points. `margin_poll` uses raw shares that include undecided respondents. FiveThirtyEight imputes some missing sample sizes; see its codebook.
- `ces_2024_likely_voters.csv`: an extract of the Cooperative Election Study Common Content, 2024 (Brian Schaffner, Marissa Shih, Stephen Ansolabehere and Jeremy Pope; Harvard Dataverse, [doi:10.7910/DVN/X11EP6](https://doi.org/10.7910/DVN/X11EP6), version 9, file `CCES24_Common_OUTPUT_vv_topost_final.csv`), retrieved 2026-10-28. Licence: CC0 1.0.
  - Rows kept: pre-election respondents who said they would definitely vote (`CC24_363` = 1), had already voted (3) or planned to vote before Election Day (4), who gave a presidential vote or preference and a 2020 vote answer. 50,117 respondents.
  - `vote_intent`: early voters' reported vote (`CC24_364a`: 1 Harris → `dem`, 2 Trump → `rep`, 3 someone else → `other`, 4 not sure → `undecided`; 5 did not vote dropped), otherwise their preference (`CC24_364b`: 1 → `dem`, 2 → `rep`, 3 → `other`, 5 not sure → `undecided`; 4 won't vote dropped).
  - `age_group`: from 2024 − `birthyr`. `education`: from `educ` (1–2 HS or less, 3–4 Some college, 5–6 College degree). `vote_2020`: from `presvote20post` (1 Biden, 2 Trump, 3–5 Other, 6 Did not vote). `ces_weight`: `commonweight`, rounded to 5 decimals. `respondent_id` is a new row number, not the CES `caseid`.
  - Targets used in the notebook. Age and education: U.S. Census Bureau, Current Population Survey, November 2024 Voting and Registration Supplement, [Table 1 and Table 5](https://www.census.gov/data/tables/time-series/demo/voting-and-registration/p20-590.html) (retrieved 2026-10-29): shares of the 154,308 thousand citizens who reported voting. Age 18–29 = single years 18–29, 30–44 = single years 30–44, 45–64 = 45–54 + 55–64, 65+ = 65–74 + 75 and over. Education: HS or less = less than 9th grade + 9th–12th no diploma + high school graduate; Some college = some college or associate's degree; College degree = bachelor's + advanced. Shares rounded to 3 decimals so each set sums to 1 (age 0.152, 0.234, 0.333, 0.281; education 0.269, 0.282, 0.449). 2020 vote: Federal Election Commission, [Official 2020 Presidential General Election Results](https://www.fec.gov/resources/cms-content/documents/2020presgeresults.pdf) (Biden 81,268,924; Trump 74,216,154; all others 2,898,325; total 158,383,403; retrieved 2026-10-29), i.e. 51.31% / 46.86% / 1.83%. Official results do not show how many 2024 voters skipped 2020, so the `Did not vote` share (0.094) is the `commonweight`-weighted share of these respondents; the remaining 0.906 is split by the official 2020 shares, assuming returning voters split like all 2020 voters. Rounded: Biden 0.465, Trump 0.424, Did not vote 0.094, Other 0.017. CES weights are post-stratified on, among other variables, age, education and 2020 and 2024 presidential vote (CES 2024 Guide, "Weighting").
